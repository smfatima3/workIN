# Results for manuscript review

After the reporting source disconnected, Qwen3-14B spilled in 34/48 simulated glass-pouring scenes with the stored position retained and 9/48 with it corrected. Correction prevented 25 spills and introduced 0. The retained-minus-corrected difference was 52.1 percentage points (95% paired interval 26.6 to 68.5). The adjusted p-value was 3.6e-07 across the six planned liquid comparisons.

A sham edit that preserved the numerical position produced 33/48 spills. Retaining the stale position changed the measured outcome for this planner and task. Qwen3-8B and Phi-4 showed smaller differences on the same layouts. Neither smaller contrast met the six-test adjusted threshold.

The same Qwen3-14B planner produced 16/48 moving-tray overloads with stale memory and 34/48 after correction. All three planners stopped in both matched bath arms. Together the results leave a general increase in unsafe action or physical injury unestablished.

The archive contains 192 of 192 conveyor scenes. For Qwen3-14B, 64 complete natural-retainer pairs gave 22 retained-only and 0 corrected-only floor contacts. The risk difference was 34.4 percentage points. The primary cohort met both prespecified directional tests. Qwen3-14B exact paired p-values were 4.8e-07 for retained versus corrected and 6e-08 for sham versus corrected. Across all valid scenes, deliveries were 42/64 with stale memory and 64/64 after correction. Qwen3-14B met the prespecified criterion on a second simulated task. The glass and conveyor studies now show a memory effect on both spatial choice and release timing. The moving-tray reversal limits broader claims.

Each rate describes the stated simulated endpoint. The conservative 95% intervals are pointwise. Numerical effect estimates and the all-enrolled sensitivity analyses are available in the evidence archives.
